I am an Assistant Professor at Indiana University Bloomington's O'Neill School of Public and Environmental Affairs, with an adjunct appointment in the Luddy School of Informatics, Computing, and Engineering. My research sits at the intersection of Information Systems, Human-Computer Interaction, and usable privacy and security. I study how people actually use and adapt digital systems when the assumptions built into those systems do not match the realities of their lives. I am particularly interested in the social and relational ways people make technologies work for their circumstances—and what these adaptations mean for trust, identity, privacy, security, access, and accountability. I use these insights to rethink how digital systems and the institutions that govern them should be designed. Prior to joining IU, I was a postdoctoral researcher at Carnegie Mellon University's CyLab Security and Privacy Institute where I was honored to be co-advised by Giulia Fanti in collaboration with Asssane Gueye. I earned my PhD in Information Systems from University of Cape Town in South Africa.

Research Areas: Information Systems . Human-computer Interaction . Usable Privacy & Security . ICT for Development (ICT4D)
